Why Is Water Essential to Life?

Water is essential to life as we know it. Molecular properties support cells but do not make life alone.

By Ahmed AlhafizPublished in Arabic: 26 September 2026English edition: 13 October 2026Independent biochemical and exegetical review: not yet completed

Direct answer

Water is essential to all known life on Earth. Its polarity, hydrogen bonding and selective dissolving behavior help cells transport substances, regulate temperature, assemble membranes, fold proteins and operate nucleic acids. Water alone is not a living system: a cell also needs boundaries, usable energy, metabolism, information, repair and regulation. The present dependence of life on water is well established; the detailed historical route by which the first life arose remains unsettled.

This essay develops pages 123–130 of Ahmed Alhafiz's Qul Siru fi al-Ard, moving from molecular structure to the working cell. The book sets out the question; independent scientific sources and Quranic exegesis support separate claims.

What does “We made from water every living thing” mean?

Quran 21:30 links a joining and parting of heavens and earth with making every living thing from water and ends by calling for belief. Al Tabari favors a reading of the heavens opening with rain and earth with plants because water follows in the verse, while also reporting other readings of their joining and parting. He interprets revivification by rain and transmits another reading about creating living things from water. Al Qurtubi discusses creation from water, continued life through it and reproductive fluid. Sustaining existing life and the historical origin of the first organism are distinct questions; the verse does not give a laboratory pathway for the latter.

Al Tabari also includes plants as living things in the relevant sense. Quran 24:45 says God created every moving creature from water and goes on to describe different modes of movement. Al Tabari reads the water there as reproductive fluid; al Qurtubi presents that and a wider connection with water in animal constitution. “Moving creature” in this context should not automatically be made identical to “every living thing” in 21:30. Neither passage names an H–O–H bond angle or a protein-folding mechanism. Chemistry can explain aspects of what water does in life without making those details the exclusive original meaning of the verse.

Why does H₂O behave as more than a formula?

Water contains one oxygen and two hydrogen atoms. Oxygen attracts shared electrons more strongly, leaving partial negative charge near it and partial positive charge near the hydrogens. The molecule is bent, roughly 104.5 degrees, so the electrical effects do not cancel. Its polarity supports a constantly changing network of hydrogen bonds between molecules.

That structure contributes to dissolving many ions and polar molecules, relatively high heat capacity and heat of vaporization, cohesion, and the lower density of ordinary ice compared with liquid water. Freezing favors a more open arrangement, so ice commonly floats. These are related physical properties, not separate supernatural explanations. Water's formula alone does not tell the whole story of liquid water's behavior.

What does water do inside a cell?

Movement, ions and signals

Water surrounds many ions and polar molecules in hydration shells, allowing transport and encounters with enzymes. In the book's salt example, water's oxygen side turns toward sodium ions while its hydrogen sides turn toward chloride ions. Sodium, potassium, calcium and chloride move in aqueous environments and their concentration differences across membranes support nerve signaling, muscle function and other processes. An action potential is a changing membrane voltage, regulated by channels and maintained by pumps and energy; it is not current in a metallic wire. Water dissolves many substances, not every substance, and that limit is crucial.

Membranes and boundaries

Cell membranes rely on phospholipids with water-interacting heads and nonpolar tails. In water they can arrange as a bilayer with heads facing the aqueous sides and tails sheltered within. The resulting dynamic barrier hosts channels, transporters and receptors. “Hydrophobic” does not mean a molecule feels fear; it describes physical interactions that favor minimizing exposure of nonpolar surfaces to water. A bilayer alone is not a living cell. Proteins and energy are needed to control crossing and maintain different conditions inside and outside.

Proteins and nucleic acids

A protein's amino-acid sequence must fold into a three-dimensional structure to work. In many soluble proteins, water-friendly groups tend to face water while nonpolar groups pack inward, but membrane proteins have different surroundings. Intramolecular bonds, ions, temperature and helper molecules also matter. Hydration water affects protein structure and motion; it is not a folding machine acting by itself.

DNA has a negatively charged phosphate backbone that interacts with water and ions. Yet a DNA molecule submerged in water is not a cell: transcription, repair, RNA production, ribosomal translation and regulation need proteins, organized boundaries and energy. Storing a sequence does not execute it.

Energy through maintained differences

ATP participates in energy-coupled processes such as active transport. Hydrolysis involves water, but usable free energy cannot be explained by a slogan about breaking a single “high-energy bond”; products, reactants and coupled reactions all matter. A published study of one ABC transporter illustrates a water molecule in a particular hydrolysis mechanism, not a universal mechanism for every machine.

In eukaryotic mitochondria, electron transport helps move protons across an inner membrane, creating a difference in both concentration and electrical potential. Proton flow through ATP synthase can then support making ATP from ADP and phosphate. The whole arrangement needs water to carry ions, a barrier to restrict their passage, proteins to control routes, and an energy input to sustain the gradient. Water performs none of those functions in isolation.

Temperature

Water's relatively high specific heat moderates temperature change, and evaporation of sweat removes energy from skin. In cold freshwater, floating ice can limit heat loss from the liquid beneath. That does not guarantee every aquatic organism survives winter, but it alters the environment compared with sinking ice.

Why does water alone not make life?

Mixing water, salts, lipids, proteins and DNA in a tube does not automatically give a working cell. A living system maintains an inside, exchanges matter and energy, builds and breaks components, reads and regulates information, repairs damage and persists. Water and DNA can remain after a cell loses the capacity to maintain those processes.

Astrobiology therefore treats water as one important sign of potential habitability. NASA's Europa Clipper explains that liquid water, suitable chemistry and energy are separate ingredients to assess. Even all three do not amount to a detection of life. The presence of water on another world answers a question about the environment, not whether organisms have been found.

How might the first life have arisen?

Existing cells demonstrate dependence on water; the earliest historical transition to life must be reconstructed from incomplete geological clues, experiments and models. Lipid vesicles or organic molecules may form in appropriate conditions, but demonstrating one possible chemical step does not establish that the actual ancient sequence followed it or that a vesicle is a modern cell.

Origin-of-life research asks how boundaries, energy capture, self-maintaining reactions and heritable information came together. Water promotes dissolution and reaction but can also hydrolyze bonds whose formation is needed in larger molecules. Experiments on wet–dry cycles, including a published polymerization result, demonstrate possible chemical pathways under specified laboratory conditions. They do not prove where or how the first life appeared. Hypotheses include changing ponds, hot springs and hydrothermal environments. Quran 21:30 identifies neither a particular site nor a genetic molecule. A precise historical account is still an open research problem.

Can life survive without water?

All known Earth life relies on liquid water during some part of its active cycle. Some tardigrades, roundworms and brine shrimp can withstand severe drying in states with greatly reduced metabolism and protective mechanisms; details differ among organisms. They resume activity when water returns. Survival during desiccation is not continuous active life independent of water. A hypothetical extraterrestrial life form using another solvent is an open possibility, not an observed counterexample.

People knew water mattered before modern science

Yes. Rain's effect on plants and the danger of thirst were familiar long before microscopes. The verse did not need molecular novelty to matter to its first audience. Modern chemistry adds mechanisms behind polarity, hydration, membranes and proteins that can enrich reflection. It does not turn a verse into a laboratory report on the first cell.

The book's contribution and an interpretive limit

Pages 123–130 build a connected sequence: molecular shape, ions, hydrogen bonds, heat and ice, water's difficulty dissolving lipids, membranes, proteins, DNA and energy. Each example serves the argument that water is an internal condition of known life, while a cell is an organized system rather than a list of ingredients.

The book also invokes Quran 27:88 about God's perfected workmanship when reflecting on protein folding. Al Tabari explains “perfected” as made firm or well ordered, but the verse's immediate context concerns mountains and the Day of the Trumpet. Applying its wider theological sense to the cell is a believer's reflection; it is not a direct detailed exegesis of protein structure.

Frequently asked questions

Why is water necessary for organisms?

It supports reactions, transport of many solutes, temperature regulation, membranes, proteins and nucleic-acid environments. It is necessary for known life, but not sufficient by itself.

How did commentators understand Quran 21:30?

They discussed creation from water, sustaining life, rain reviving plants and reproductive water. The verse does not explicitly teach molecular chemistry.

Is water a universal solvent?

No. It dissolves many ionic and polar substances but does not readily dissolve nonpolar lipids; that contrast helps membranes form.

Does finding water prove life exists?

No. Suitable chemistry, energy and an independently detected living system would still matter.

Do tardigrades live without water?

They can survive drying in dormancy and become active again after rehydration. That is different from active water-free life.

Does the verse say that life necessarily began in the sea?

No. It names neither the first environment nor the chemical route of life's origin.

Evidence transparency

Experiments and molecular reviews support mechanisms in living cells and bounded prebiotic possibilities. They do not establish a complete historical origin of life. Exegesis grounds the scriptural meanings, and the book organizes the questions.
